Why this exists

A Swiggy rider and an Uber driver are often the same person

More than half of gig workers run two or more apps at once. Every platform sees only its own slice — its own earnings, its own hours, its own map. Nobody sees the whole day, least of all the person working it.

What a rider actually has

Four apps that do not talk to each other, a number at the end of the week they cannot check, and no one to ask about the road ahead.

What Waggle adds

One place where the distance is counted honestly, the map has the people you know on it, and the road report comes from riders who came through ten minutes ago.

What it costs a rider

Nothing, and there are no ads. Not a trial. The people who use this are the ones who can least afford another subscription.

Where it stands today

Closed testing, version 1.0, Android 7.0 and later. Install it directly below. It is not on Google Play publicly yet, and we would rather say that than imply otherwise.

Built end to end at Gigzen and tested by Populace, our own tool, which drove thirty-six simulated riders through 7,485 API calls with no failures — after an earlier run found six real defects this app's own author had missed. The full record, including what was never tested.

Earnings

Money that follows the road

Earnings accrue from distance genuinely travelled. Sitting in traffic does not inflate the number, because a stationary phone earns nothing. The day's total resets at midnight, in your own currency, and you can see how it was arrived at rather than being handed a figure.

The map

A map with people on it

Watch the riders you know move in real time, and search any city, neighbourhood or shop on earth. Sharing your position is off until you turn it on, and only people you have added as friends can see it — an empty map is the default, not something you have to go and configure.

Community

The people working your streets

Flooding, surges, closures, queues — told by the riders who just came through them, not by a dispatcher three cities away. Posts, photos, reels and group chats, with the same message controls you already expect: delete for yourself, or delete for everyone.

Reach

Built for wherever the work is

Language and currency come from where the rider actually is. Right-to-left layouts included, not bolted on.

Built to survive a real day

A delivery shift is not a good network

It keeps working offline

Posts and messages written with no signal are queued and sent when the connection returns, rather than lost with an error you notice an hour later.

Your location is yours

Off by default. Visible only to friends you added. Turning it off stops the sharing immediately — there is no background exception.

Tested by a simulated crowd

Before release it was driven by Populace, our own testing tool, which runs dozens of simulated riders through the real API at once. Its first run found five bugs that single-user testing had missed; the latest put ten riders through 2,116 API calls with none. Read the full report — it lists what was not tested too.

Account deletion actually works

The path most apps never exercise. You can delete your account from inside the app, and it removes your data rather than hiding it.
